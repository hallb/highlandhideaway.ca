The Highlands do not turn all at once. The colour arrives over about three
weeks and moves across the county as it goes, which is worth knowing if you
are trying to time a visit.

## Why leaves change

The leaves are not turning so much as being emptied. Chlorophyll is the
green pigment a tree uses to make sugar, and it masks everything else in
the leaf all summer. As the days shorten and the nights cool, the tree
stops making sugar and pulls the chlorophyll back out to store for spring.
The yellows and oranges underneath, carotenes and xanthophylls, were there
the whole time. What changes is what is covering them.

The reds are different. Anthocyanins are made late in the season rather
than uncovered, and are thought to shade the leaf from bright light while
the tree finishes reclaiming what it wants from it.

Day length is the trigger that does not vary, which is why the turn lands
in roughly the same fortnight every year. Weather moves it a little either
way. A warm September holds it back, a cold snap brings it on, and a dry
summer dulls the colour when it comes.

## What turns, and when

The mix below is the normal one for the Highlands. Any given hillside
carries some of it and not the rest.

Red maple goes first, and goes scarlet. It grows on wet ground, so the low
ends of things turn before the high ends.

Sugar maple follows, orange through red. This is the one people picture
when they say fall colours, and it is the tree the county's peak week is
measured by.

Then the gold: birch, aspen, and tamarack, roughly a week behind the
maples. Tamarack is worth watching for. It is a conifer that drops its
needles every year, and it goes a clear gold late in the season, after most
of the maple leaves are down.

Red oak is last, brownish red through to deep scarlet.

So there is no single day when the county is at its best. There is a
fortnight of maples and then a fortnight of gold, and the second one is
quieter and much less busy.

## Why the turn is staggered

Three things spread it out.

Height is the first. Cold settles into low ground overnight and drains off
the high ground, and the higher country is colder to begin with, so height
turns early. Algonquin, which is both higher and further north, averages
its maple peak in the last week of September, ahead of the county.

Wet ground is the second, for the same reason and because it holds the red
maple that goes first anyway.

Aspect is the third. A slope facing south spends the afternoon in sun and
holds its green longer than the shaded side of the same hill.

The practical effect is that the turn is spread over about three weeks
across the Highlands rather than arriving and leaving in a weekend. Early
in the season, the colour is north and high. Late in it, the colour is
south and low. If you come slightly early or slightly late, there is
usually still somewhere worth driving to.

## Timing it

We are projecting peak colour here around **15 October 2026**.

Treat that as a projection and not a promise. For the county as a whole,
the maples usually run through the first twelve days of October and the
gold follows from about the 13th to the 21st, so a mid-October visit tends
to catch the end of the one and the start of the other.

Year to year this moves by a week in either direction, and it is decided by
weather nobody can see from here in August. Anyone telling you the exact
weekend six months out is guessing.

Two reports are updated through the season and are better than this page
once the season starts:

- [The Ontario Parks fall colour report](https://www.ontarioparks.ca/fallcolour),
  updated weekly, covers Algonquin, Silent Lake, and Arrowhead. Those are
  the three nearest parks and the closest thing to a reading for here.
- [The Algonquin fall colours report](https://algonquinpark.com/ontario-fall-colours-report/)
  carries a prediction map for the wider province.

Check one of them in the week before you travel.

## Where to look from

### Skyline Park

The lookout at [Skyline Park](https://1000towns.ca/skyline-park/) is the
one to do first, and it is ten minutes from the cottage. It sits on the
hill just east of the village at 196 Skyline Road, off Highway 118, and it
looks out over the village, over Head Lake, and a long way into the
Highlands behind it. In the first half of October that view is most of the
county's colour in one frame.

Ten minutes is short enough that it does not need to be an outing. Go before
breakfast, or go at the end of the day. The lookout faces back west over the
village, and it has a reputation for sunsets.

You drive to it. Skyline Road runs to the lookout and stops there, so there
is no walk in unless you want one. There are picnic tables at the top and
the park is free.

If you do want the walk, AllTrails has the short loop at the top as
[Skyline Road Lookout](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/canada/ontario/skyline-road-lookout):
about 300 metres, roughly ten metres of climb, and easy. It is a stretch of
the legs after the drive rather than a hike.

### From the water

The view most people do not think of is from a lake. A far shore reads as
one unbroken band of colour from out on the water, and you cannot see it
that way from land.

{{< image src="/images/dock-autumn.jpg" alt="A wooden dock reaching into calm water with autumn colour on the far shore" caption="The far shore, from the launch on Drag Lake" >}}

The public launch on Drag Lake is across the road from us, and there are
launches on most of the lakes around the village. See the
[canoe launch guide](/posts/canoe-launch-guide/). October paddling is cold
water paddling, so go out dressed for falling in.

### From the road

The county roads do most of the work on their own. The stretches worth
going out of your way for are the ones that climb, because you end up
looking across the tops of the trees rather than through the trunks.

## Booking it

Peak books out before it arrives, not after. If you want the maples, the
first two weekends of October are the ones to hold, and the Studio Tour and
Thanksgiving both fall inside that window. See
[the Haliburton Studio Tour](/posts/haliburton-studio-tour/) and
[Thanksgiving at the Hideaway](/posts/canadian-thanksgiving/).

If you would rather have the gold and the quiet, come the week after.
