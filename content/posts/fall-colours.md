The turn of the fall colours from summer verdance to autumnal fireworks is staggered rather than all at once, which is worth knowing
if you're trying to time a visit.

## Why leaves change

The leaves are not turning so much as being emptied. Chlorophyll is the
green pigment a tree uses to make sugar, and it masks everything else in
the leaf all summer. As the days shorten and the nights cool, the tree
stops making sugar and pulls the chlorophyll back out to store for spring.
The yellows and oranges underneath, carotenes and xanthophylls, were there
the whole time. What changes is what is covering them.

The reds are different. Anthocyanins are made late in the season rather
than uncovered, and are thought to shade the leaf from bright light while
the tree finishes reclaiming what it wants from it.

Day length is the trigger that does not vary, which is why the turn lands
in roughly the same fortnight every year. Weather moves it a little either
way. A warm September holds it back, a cold snap brings it on, and a dry
summer dulls the colour when it comes.

## What turns, and when

Red maple goes first, and goes scarlet. It grows on the wet ground, which
is part of why the low end of the property turns before the rest.

Sugar maple follows, orange through red. This is the one people picture
when they say fall colours, and it is the tree the county's peak week is
measured by.

Then the gold: birch, aspen, and tamarack, roughly a week behind the
maples. Tamarack is worth watching for. It is a conifer that drops its
needles every year, and it goes a clear gold late in the season, after
most of the maple leaves are down.

Red oak is last, brownish red through to deep scarlet.

So there is no single day when the property is at its best. There is a
fortnight of maples and then a fortnight of gold, and the second one is
quieter and much less busy.

## Why the property doesn't turn all at once

Most of the 23 acres is mixed forest, and it does not change as one block.

The low wet ground turns first. It holds red maple, and wet feet and cold
air both settle at the bottom of a slope. The high dry ground behind the
red shed turns later. South-facing ground holds its green longer than
ground that spends the afternoon in shade.

The practical effect is that the turn is spread over about two weeks here
rather than arriving and leaving in a weekend. If you come slightly early
or slightly late, there is usually still something worth walking to.

## Timing it

We are projecting peak colour here around **15 October 2026**.

Treat that as a projection and not a promise. For the county as a whole,
the maples usually run through the first twelve days of October and the
gold follows from about the 13th to the 21st, so a mid-October visit tends
to catch the end of the one and the start of the other. Algonquin, which
is higher and further north, averages its maple peak in the last week of
September.

Year to year this moves by a week in either direction, and it is decided by
weather we cannot see from here in September. Anyone telling you the exact
weekend six months out is guessing.

Two reports are updated through the season and are better than this page
once the season starts:

- [The Ontario Parks fall colour report](https://www.ontarioparks.ca/fallcolour),
  updated weekly, covers Algonquin, Silent Lake, and Arrowhead. Those are
  the three nearest parks and the closest thing to a reading for here.
- [The Algonquin fall colours report](https://algonquinpark.com/ontario-fall-colours-report/)
  carries a prediction map for the wider province.

Check one of them in the week before you travel.

## Where to look from

From the grounds, the edge of the clearing is the easiest look. The maple
the swing hangs from is at that edge.

The blue-blazed trail behind the red shed climbs the hill, and the height
is the point: from partway up you are looking across the tops of the trees
rather than through the trunks. See [the grounds](/posts/the-grounds/).

The view most people don't think of is from the water. The far shore of
Drag Lake reads as one unbroken band of colour from out on the lake, and
you cannot see it that way from land. The public launch is across the road.

## Booking it

Peak books out before it arrives, not after. If you want the maples, the
first two weekends of October are the ones to hold, and the Studio Tour and
Thanksgiving both fall inside that window. See
[the Haliburton Studio Tour](/posts/haliburton-studio-tour/) and
[Thanksgiving at the Hideaway](/posts/canadian-thanksgiving/).

If you would rather have the gold and the quiet, come the week after.
