Not every day here is a lake day. When the weather is wrong for the water
and wrong for the woods, a film is a reasonable answer. There is a screen
in the living room, and there are three places to see one properly: a
monthly film night ten minutes away, a cinema in Kinmount through the
warm months, and a festival in November.

## At the cottage

The living room has a TV, and the cottage has internet that will stream.
Bring your own login for whatever service you use. We do not keep
subscriptions on the TV.

The connection is not high bandwidth. One film at a time is fine. Several
devices streaming at once can overload it.

Light the wood stove first. It takes about twenty minutes to make a real
difference to the room. See
[lighting the wood stove](/posts/lighting-the-wood-stove/) and
[a cozy night in](/posts/cozy-night-in/), which also covers the board
games and the bookshelf for when nobody can agree on a film.

## Thursdays with TOM, monthly

Those Other Movies runs a film night on the second Thursday of most
months at the Northern Lights Performing Arts Pavilion in Haliburton,
ten minutes from the cottage. One film, screened twice, at 4:15pm and
7:15pm. Tickets are sold per screening.

It is a curated programme rather than whatever is on release. Canadian
and international work, often subtitled, chosen one film at a time. The
films announced for the rest of 2026:

- **Thursday 10 September.** *I Swear* (Kirk Jones, 2025). Based on the
  life of the Tourette's activist John Davidson. Robert Aramayo won a
  BAFTA for playing him. Shown with subtitles.
- **Thursday 8 October.** *You Had To Be There* (Nick Davis, 2025). A
  documentary about how a Toronto run of *Godspell* in the early 1970s
  happened to contain Martin Short, Eugene Levy, Gilda Radner, Andrea
  Martin and Paul Shaffer, and what that did to North American comedy
  afterwards.
- **Thursday 10 December.** *Once Upon A Time in a Cinema* (David
  Gleeson, 2026). One night in a failing small-town Irish cinema, told
  in real time.

There is no Thursday night in November. The festival takes that month.

The series picks up again in the new year, and there is a documentary
day, Doc(k) Day, on Saturday 3 April 2027, running 10am to 10pm at the
same venue. Dates and titles for 2027 are on the same page as the
tickets:
[thoseothermovies.com/thursdayswithtom](https://www.thoseothermovies.com/thursdayswithtom).

## Highlands Cinemas, Kinmount

Highlands Cinemas is a five-screen cinema at 4131 County Road 121,
outside Kinmount, and it is built into a house. It is a 40 minute drive
from us, so it is an evening you decide on in the morning rather than at
six o'clock. Keith Stata started it in 1979 with one small room in his
own home, and added a screen at a time until there were five. The
projection and seating came out of cinemas that were closing across
North America as he found them, which is also how the movie museum on
the site was filled.

There are 58 cats living on the property. They are not a gimmick; they
are cats that needed somewhere to go, and the theatre takes donations for
them. Somebody made a documentary about the place called *The Movie Man*.

It shows first-run films. Two practical things to know before you drive
there:

- **Cash only.** No debit, no credit.
- **The doors open 30 minutes before the first posted show**, and not
  before.

Admission includes the museum. Adults are $13, children 12 and under and
seniors 65 and up are $11, matinees are $9, and Monday nights are $9. The
museum on its own is $6. All of those include tax.

It is seasonal. It opens around the start of May and closes in the
autumn, and their own account of the season puts the end of it around
Thanksgiving. They had not published a 2026 closing date when this was
written, and matinees have already finished for the year, so by
September the listings are evening shows only. Check the schedule at
[highlandscinemas.com](https://www.highlandscinemas.com/) before you go,
or phone the hotline on 705 488 2107.

## The film festival, 6 to 8 November 2026

The Haliburton International Film Festival runs Friday 6 to Sunday 8
November 2026 at the Northern Lights Performing Arts Pavilion in
Haliburton. Three days and seven films. The 2026 lineup had not been
announced when this was written.

HIFF is run by Those Other Movies, the TIFF Film Circuit programme for
Haliburton County, and it has run since 2007. The films are the sort that
otherwise mean a drive to Toronto: Canadian features, subtitled European
and international work, and the occasional large release. Directors and
crew turn up to talk about them. The 2025 festival had the producers of a
Canadian crime drama in to explain how a Canadian film gets made, which
is not a thing the multiplex offers.

For the shape of a weekend, 2025 is the best guide there is until the
2026 schedule appears. It ran one film on the Friday, four on the
Saturday starting at 10am and finishing with a 7:30pm screening, and two
on the Sunday, followed by a wrap party at 3pm with snacks and drinks.
Note that 2025 opened in Kinmount and moved to Haliburton for the
weekend. In 2026 everything is at the pavilion.

### Tickets

Three ways in:

- **VIP Front of the Line pass, $65.** Every film, all weekend.
- **Day pass, $32.** Unlimited screenings on one day.
- **Single tickets, $10 a film.** A limited number of rush tickets at the
  door, sold in person only. In-person sales open at 9am on Saturday 7
  November 2026.

Passes go on sale closer to the festival, through Zeffy. Zeffy is built
for non-profits and charges no service fee to either side, so you pay
the listed price and nothing on top. It will ask you for a donation at
checkout, which is optional; choose "Other" if you want to change the
amount.

Everything official, including the lineup when it lands, is at
[thoseothermovies.com/hiff](https://www.thoseothermovies.com/hiff). We
have no connection to the festival.

## Staying for it

The festival is a full weekend if you want it to be, and the first film
on the Saturday starts at 10am. That is an early drive from anywhere that
is not Haliburton County.

The pavilion is ten minutes from us. Early November is quiet here, the
lake is still open, and it is cold enough that the wood stove is worth
lighting. See [what is nearby](/posts/whats-nearby/) for groceries and
the practical geography.
