Winter is the quietest season here, and the one the cottage was built for.

## Getting in

The driveway is steep, and it is ploughed within a day of a snowfall. You
will need all-wheel drive and snow tires to get up it. If you cannot, park
near the bottom, clear of the neighbours, and walk up.

Arrive before dark on your first day if you can. See
[getting here and parking](/posts/getting-here-and-parking/).

## Heat

The baseboard heaters do the work; the wood stove supplements them in the
living room. Firewood for the stove is stacked on the deck by the sliding
door and under the roof of the red shed. The deck stack is the easier of
the two to reach once there is snow on the ground.

{{< image src="/images/woodshed-winter.jpg" alt="The red shed with firewood stacked under its roof, half buried in snow" caption="The red shed in February" >}}

See [lighting the wood stove](/posts/lighting-the-wood-stove/).

## The Sanctuary in winter

The screened deck stays in use through the cold months. Coffee happens out
there most mornings of the year, stopping only for the coldest ones.

{{< image src="/images/sanctuary-winter.jpg" alt="Paper lanterns strung across the screened deck, snow on the ground beyond" caption="The Sanctuary in March" >}}

## Outside

The blue-blazed trail behind the red shed is good for snowshoeing, and the
forest is silent in a way it never is in summer.

{{< image src="/images/forest-trail-winter.jpg" alt="A snow-covered trail through bare trees under a blue sky" caption="The trail behind the cottage" >}}

The lake freezes hard enough for tracks across it.

{{< image src="/images/lake-frozen.jpg" alt="A frozen lake with snowmobile tracks running into the distance" caption="Drag Lake in February" >}}

Ice is never guaranteed. Check with someone local before walking out on it,
and do not take a vehicle onto it.

## What to bring

Boots, layers, and something for the dark. Sunset in January is before five.
