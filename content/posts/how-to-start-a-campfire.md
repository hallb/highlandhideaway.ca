The fire pit is behind the cottage. The wood stacked beside it is the
campfire wood. Dry kindling is under the roof of the red shed, and there
are fire starters in the grey box next to the TV.

## Check the fire ban first

Haliburton County posts fire restrictions through the summer. [Check before
you light anything](https://www.dysartetal.ca/living-in-our-community/fire-and-emergency-services/).

## Building it

1. Clear the ash from the last fire to one side. Air needs to get under the
   fire, not just around it.
2. Put a loose handful of paper or birch bark in the middle.
3. Build a small cone of kindling over it, leaving gaps.
4. Light it, then leave it alone. The most common mistake is adding wood
   too early.
5. When the kindling is burning hard, add two splits leaning against each
   other. Add more only once those have caught.

## When the wood is damp

In spring the outer stack takes on moisture. Split a piece and use the dry
inner faces as kindling. A damp fire needs more kindling than you think and
more patience than you want.

## Putting it out

Douse it with water, stir the ash, and douse it again. If it is warm enough
to hold your hand near, it is not out.

Never leave a fire burning when you go inside for the night.

## Nearby

The benches around the pit seat six or so. It is the best spot on the
property once the sun drops.
