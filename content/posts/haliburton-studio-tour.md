Last October we spent an afternoon at Artech Studios, a traditional glass
blowing studio in the Haliburton Highlands. They ran a demonstration in front
of the furnace while they made a variety of ornaments. The cozy workshop was
warm against the autumn chill outside, and it ended with marshmallows toasted
for us in a non-standard use of their furnace.

Their retail shop sells upcycled brown beer bottles turned into drinking
glasses, among other things. And I now have some brown glass fridge magnets.

That was one stop on the Studio Tour, which runs for two weekends every
autumn.

## Why is there so much art here?

The Highlands has a working arts community rather than a gift-shop version
of one. People live here and make things here, and a good number of them
will talk to you about their work if you turn up.

The Haliburton School of Fine Arts started in 1967. Its first courses ran at
Royal View Lodge on Lake Kashagawigamog, before it had a building of its
own. It became an extension division of Sir Sandford Fleming College shortly
after. The campus moved to Glebe Park, above Head Lake, in 2004. It's now the
Haliburton School of Art + Design.

The school grew out of a local arts community that was already there.

I've taken a course there myself, a week of timber framing under a pavilion
on the campus grounds. You'll see timber framed gazebos all around Haliburton
once you start looking. The one my class built is at St. Stephen's Cemetery,
near Boshkung and Halls Lakes.

Glebe Park also holds the Haliburton Sculpture Forest, with more than
twenty-five permanent works along the trails. It's free and open year-round,
and it fills an hour without any planning.

## How the Studio Tour works

The tour is self-guided. You drive it yourself, in whatever order suits you,
and the studios are open 10am to 5pm on both weekends:

- Saturday 3 and Sunday 4 October 2026
- Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October 2026

It runs roughly east to west, from Tory Hill to Carnarvon, across over
twenty-five studios. The work covers pottery and ceramics, glass, fine
jewellery, textile and fabric art, painting, leatherwork, photography,
quilting, and metal art.

Some of these studios are only open to visitors on these two weekends of the
year, so that worth planning around.

For the map, the studio and artist list, and the latest information, see
[thestudiotour.ca](https://www.thestudiotour.ca/).

## Booking it

The second tour weekend is also the Thanksgiving long weekend,
Saturday 10 to Monday 12 October. You can do the tour on the Saturday or
Sunday and still have the Monday. See
[Thanksgiving at the Hideaway](/posts/canadian-thanksgiving/).

We're a ten minute drive from the village of Haliburton, which puts most of
the tour within easy reach.
