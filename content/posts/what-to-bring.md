The cottage is stocked for cooking and sleeping. Most of what people forget
is small.

## Already here

- Two pillows on each bed, and duvets or blankets in the side and corner
  room closets
- Pots, pans, dishes, cutlery, and basic kitchen tools
- Salt, pepper, oil, and a few staples
- Firewood, kindling, and fire starters
- Toilet paper, hand soap, and body soap

## Bring

- Towels, and queen-sized linens: fitted and flat sheets, pillow cases, and
  duvet covers. All three beds are queens. This is the one thing people are
  caught out by, so see [sleeping arrangements](/posts/sleeping-arrangements/).
- Groceries. Haliburton is less than ten minutes away and has two full
  grocery stores, so a forgotten item is an inconvenience rather than a
  disaster. See the [pantry guide](/posts/cottage-pantry-guide/).
- Child-sized life jackets, if you are bringing children. The life jackets
  at the cottage are adult-sized.
- Insect repellent from mid-May through July. The screened-in deck helps,
  but the trails do not.
- Layers. Nights are cold well into June.
- Footwear you do not mind getting muddy.
- Anything you need for a specific dietary requirement.

## No need to bring

Paddles, adult life jackets, and the safety kit are in the utility room,
along with a cart for getting a boat down to the launch. Ground coffee is
provided. See the [canoe launch guide](/posts/canoe-launch-guide/).

Nor much to keep you occupied. There is a TV you can sign into with your own
streaming account, a CD player and around 200 CDs, board games, and a shelf
of books. See [a cozy night in](/posts/cozy-night-in/).

## Worth bringing

- A headlamp. There are no streetlights and the path to the fire pit is
  uneven.
- A cooler, if you are coming in summer and plan a day out.
- Your own pillow, if you are particular about pillows.

## Leave at home

Fireworks and floating lanterns.

No pets, no smoking.
