## 1. Context

### What this is for

The client operates a short-term rental (STR) property and is building out a **direct booking website** to reduce dependency on OTA platforms (currently Airbnb). Part of that strategy is content-driven local SEO: publishing pages that rank for hyper-local queries and funnel readers toward a direct booking CTA.

The specific task: the client has a **fall colours post already drafted** (not seen by me — see §5) and asked whether an upcoming local event, the Stanhope Municipal Airport Fall Colours Fly-In, should be folded into that existing post or published as its own piece.

The end reader is a prospective guest planning an autumn trip to the Haliburton Highlands region of Ontario, Canada. Two overlapping audiences:

1. People searching seasonally ("Haliburton fall colours," "when is peak colour Ontario") — broad, early-funnel.
2. People searching for this specific named event — narrow, late-funnel, higher intent, and some fraction of them will need accommodation for the night before.

## 6. Decisions already made, and why

### Decision 1 — Publish as a **separate post**, do not merge into the existing fall colours piece. *(Decided)*

Reasoning, so it doesn't get re-argued:

- **The two pieces serve different search intents.** The fall colours post targets broad seasonal/destination queries and is evergreen — refreshed each year, works as the conversion piece. The fly-in post targets a named-event query with a fixed date. One page cannot have a title tag, URL, and H1 optimised for both.
- **Competition on the event query is thin.** The main result is the township's own page. A small operator can realistically rank for it.
- **Merging degrades both.** Time-boxed event detail bloats the evergreen guide and goes stale on 27 September; the event content never gets a URL or title matching what people search.
- **Hyper-local event content is comparatively resistant to AI-generated answer summaries** displacing the click, versus generic informational content — this was the client's own working principle and it fits here. *(This is a strategic rationale, [INFERRED], not a measured finding.)*

**Rejected:** folding the event into the fall colours post as a subsection. Rejected for the reasons above. Do not revisit without new information.

### Decision 2 — Use a **permanent, non-year-stamped URL**. *(Decided)*

`/stanhope-fall-colours-fly-in`, **not** `/fly-in-2026`. The event recurs annually (confirmed 2023, 2024, 2026). A stable URL updated each year accumulates authority; a new URL every year restarts from zero and leaves dead pages behind.

**Rejected:** year-stamped URLs, and a fresh post per year.

### Decision 3 — Add **Event schema markup**. *(Decided)*

Date, location, and free-admission fields. Municipal sites frequently omit this, so it is a low-cost differentiator for event rich results. Contingent on the CMS supporting it (unverified, §5).

### Decision 4 — The **Friday night is the booking hook**. *(Decided in principle, contingent)*

The flyer indicates overnight camping on 25 September, which establishes that attendees are already planning to arrive the night before. That is the wedge: a "prefer a bed to a tent?" angle the township's page will never make.

**Contingent on** confirming the pilots-only restriction (§3) and the property's proximity and availability (§5). If camping is pilots-only, the angle still works but must be reframed — it becomes "attendees travelling in for a 10 a.m. start" rather than "campers who'd rather not camp."

### Decision 5 — **Cross-link in both directions**. *(Decided)*

The evergreen fall colours guide gets a short "while you're here" mention linking to the event post. The event post links back to the guide and to the booking page. Event post captures the search; the guide does the conversion work.

- Word count, tone, and voice — depends on the existing draft, which I have not seen.
- Whether to publish before or after resolving the flyer's year (§5). My view is resolve first, but the client may have direct knowledge.

## 7. Deliverable structure

Proposed outline for the standalone event post. Reasoning given per section so it can be adapted rather than followed blindly.

**Title / H1** — something containing the event's actual name and the year, e.g. "Stanhope Fall Colours Fly-In 2026: What to Know." *Why:* the searcher is typing the event name; the title must contain it. Year in the title (but not the URL) so the page reads as current while the URL stays permanent.

**Section 1 — The essentials, up top, scannable.**
Date, time, venue, address, admission cost. Four or five lines, possibly a small table. *Why:* the dominant intent behind an event query is logistics. Anyone who has to scroll past three paragraphs of scene-setting to find the start time bounces. This section also carries the facts the schema markup mirrors.

**Section 2 — What's on.**
The feature list, written as prose or a short list rather than dumped verbatim from the flyer. *Why:* differentiates from the township page, which is terse. **Only 2026-confirmed features** — see conflict C3.

**Section 3 — The scenic flights.**
Short, its own section. *Why:* this is the emotional hook and the most distinctive thing about the event — foliage from the air. It is also likely a secondary search query in its own right. Do not state prices unless confirmed.

**Section 4 — Getting there and practicalities.**
Driving directions context, parking, what to bring, weather. *Why:* genuinely useful, and it is where the geography naturally establishes where the property sits relative to the airport — without a hard sell.

**Section 5 — Staying the night.**
The camping note (with its pilots-only qualifier if confirmed), then the accommodation angle and the direct booking CTA. *Why:* placed after the reader has decided they're going, not before. Late placement converts better on informational-intent pages and avoids the piece reading as an advert.

**Section 6 — More autumn in the Highlands.**
Two or three sentences linking to the evergreen fall colours guide. *Why:* passes the event page's traffic to the conversion page, and gives readers who arrived for the event a reason to keep reading.

**Below the fold / footer** — a line pointing to the township's official page as the authoritative source for changes. *Why:* honest, useful, and it protects the client if details change after publication.

**Schema markup** — Event type, matching Section 1 exactly.

## 8. Constraints and rules

**Tone.** Useful and local, not promotional. The page earns its ranking by being the most helpful version of the event listing. A hard sell in the first half undermines that.

**Do not invent.** Specifically:
- No feature, vendor, performer, aircraft, or attraction that is not confirmed for 2026. Prior-year sources mention a brewery beer garden and firefighting equipment displays; **these are 2024 details and must not appear as 2026 facts.**
- No prices for scenic flights, food, or anything else, unless confirmed from a current source.
- No attendance figures. None were found.
- No claims about peak foliage timing stated as fact.

**Do not claim.**
- Any affiliation with, sponsorship by, or endorsement from the Township of Algonquin Highlands, the airport, or the Airport Advisory Committee. There is none. This is third-party coverage of a public event.
- That the event is confirmed to run rain or shine — unverified.
- That camping is open to general visitors — the flyer says pilots.
- Any proximity claim ("minutes from the airport") until the distance is actually measured.

**Personal contact details.** The Airport Coordinator is a named individual and the two published numbers for her disagree (C1). Prefer linking to the township's contact page over republishing her direct email and phone in your own content. Lower risk, and it stays correct if she changes roles.

**Things that would be embarrassing if wrong**, in rough order of severity:
1. Wrong date or year. Readers plan travel around this.
2. Telling readers they can camp when they can't.
3. Listing attractions that don't exist in 2026.
4. Implying an official relationship with the township.
5. Wrong contact number for a named municipal employee.

## 9. Time sensitivity

- **Written 22 August 2026. The event is 26 September 2026 — roughly five weeks out.**
- **Publish soon.** Event-query search volume typically climbs through the two weeks before the date. Pages published inside the final week rarely get indexed and ranked in time to catch it. Earlier is materially better here.
- **The page stops being useful on 27 September 2026.** At that point it should be converted — either to a recap with a "next year's date TBA" line, or updated with the 2027 date as soon as the township announces it. Do not leave a stale 2026 date on a permanent URL.
- **Aged sources:** anything in this brief marked 2023 or 2024 is between two and three years old and should be re-checked before being relied upon. The OurAirports material is from 2007–2009 and should not be used at all.
- **Peak foliage timing** is weather-dependent and varies year to year. If the post references it, source it from a current provincial fall colour report near the publication date, not from memory or from a prior year's page.
