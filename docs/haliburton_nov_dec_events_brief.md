## 3. Source conflicts — must be resolved before publishing

These are real, unreconciled disagreements. I have not chosen a winner in any of them, and the incoming agent should not either without going back to a primary source.

### 3.1 Christmas Market times: 10:00am–3:00pm vs 9:00am–2:00pm

The tourism calendar **displays** 10:00am–3:00pm. The same listing's embedded calendar-export parameters are `20261121T140000Z` to `20261121T190000Z`, which convert to **9:00am–2:00pm Eastern Standard Time** on that date. These differ by one hour.

Most likely explanation is a timezone-handling bug in the calendar plugin, in which case the displayed 10:00–3:00 is correct. **That is a guess, not a finding.** Confirm against the Facebook event or the organiser before publishing any time at all.

### 3.2 Christmas Market hours and scope: 10:00am–3:00pm vs 11:00am–5:00pm

The holiday-markets page describes a market running 10:00am–3:00pm at A.J. LaRue Arena with no programming detail. A separate tourism-site event page (snippet, ~Nov 2024) describes a much larger event: 11:00am–5:00pm, spanning **both** the arena and the Royal Canadian Legion, with Santa's Workshop, cookie decorating, cartoon screenings and a toy drive.

Three possibilities, none eliminated:
- These are the same annual event and it has shrunk (or the newer listing is simply less detailed).
- These are two different events in the same season at overlapping venues.
- The 2024 listing describes the "Winter Fair & Christmas Market" (see §2.4), which may be a separate, larger event.

The existence of a **second Facebook event ID** for a similarly-named market compounds this. **[UNRESOLVED]** — do not publish a description of what happens at this market until the scope question is settled.

### 3.3 Parade dates: internally inconsistent, apparently mixing years

The holiday-markets page lists three parades with day-of-week/date pairs:

| Listing | Page says | Consistency check |

| Christmas in the Village, Village of Minden — Santa parade 11:00am, then artisans market, baked goods and candy at the Cultural Centre | **Sat, Nov 15** | Matches 2025 (Sat). Does **not** match 2026 — 15 Nov 2026 is a Sunday |
| The Colours of Christmas, Village of Haliburton — bands, horses, marchers, carolling and tree lighting | **Fri, Nov 22** | Matches **2024** (Fri). Does not match 2025 (Sat) or 2026 (Sun) |

So the page is not merely out of date; it appears to contain entries from **at least two different years simultaneously**. **None of these three dates can be used for 2026.** The event *names*, *locations* and *descriptions* are probably still reliable; the dates are not. ## 5. Proposed deliverable structure

Four posts were proposed. **None was approved.** The reasoning is given so the incoming agent can argue with it rather than guess at it.

### Post A — "Haliburton Christmas Market 2026: What to Know Before You Go"

*Why this one:* It is the only event with a defensible 2026 date, and it is a query with clear intent ("when is X", "where is X") that a visitor types shortly before travelling. It also carries two details — stairs-only access and an unresolved question of what actually happens at the market — that a generic AI-generated answer will not have. Specificity is the moat.

| Section | Content | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Answer block (opening 2–3 sentences) | Date, time, venue, address, cost if known | Search intent here is factual and impatient. Bury the answer and the visitor leaves. Also gives the passage the best chance of being the source an AI answer summary cites. |
| Getting there and parking | Directions, parking reality | The detail nobody publishes and everybody needs. |
| Accessibility | The stairs constraint, stated plainly | Genuinely useful, and differentiating — **only if re-verified**. |
| What to expect | Vendors, food, family programming | **Cannot be written until §3.2 is resolved.** Leave as a stub rather than guessing. |
| Making a day of it | Nearby food and other things open that weekend | Extends dwell time and moves the reader from event-lookup toward trip-planning. |
| Stay nearby | Direct booking CTA | The conversion point. Should read as a practical suggestion, not an ad break. |

### Post B — "HIFF Weekend Without the Drive Home"

*Why this one:* The 2025 festival's own website acknowledged that attendees face a long drive to the Kinmount opening venue. That is a genuine, festival-acknowledged logistics problem, and accommodation is a genuine answer to it — so the commercial angle is not a stretch. This is the strongest natural fit between an event and the client's product.

| Section | Content | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| What HIFF is | Brief framing: TIFF Film Circuit, small-town festival, milestone 20th season | Most searchers won't know. Establishes the event is worth travelling for. |
| The venue-split problem | The two-venue geography and what it means for a day-tripper | This is the whole premise. Must be re-verified for 2026 — if the split doesn't repeat, the post loses its spine and should be rewritten or dropped. |
| Weekend shape | How Fri/Sat/Sun realistically plays out | Practical value; also naturally surfaces the overnight need. |
| Tickets | Passes vs single tickets, where to buy | Useful, and links out to the festival — a genuine signal of local usefulness rather than extraction. |
| Where to stay | Direct booking CTA | Lands as a solution to a problem the post established honestly. |

### Post C — Two roundup pages: "Haliburton Highlands Events: November 2026" and "…December 2026"

*Why this one:* Roundups capture the broad, undifferentiated queries ("things to do in Haliburton in November") that individual event posts miss, and they internally link to Posts A and B. Two separate pages rather than one combined page, because November and December are different search behaviours: November skews to parades and the film festival, December to markets and Christmas.

*Structural decision:* **Update these pages in place each year; do not publish a new post annually.** A single URL accumulating authority beats a fragmented archive of dated posts. Show a visible "last updated" date to keep it trustworthy.

| Section | Content | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Chronological listing | One entry per event: date, time, place, one line of description, outbound link | Scannability is the entire value. Resist writing essays per entry. |
| Standing/recurring items | Winter activities that aren't one-off events | Fills gaps for readers whose dates don't match any event. |
| Practical notes | Weather, road conditions, what closes for the season | The kind of local knowledge that earns links and repeat visits. |
| Stay | Direct booking CTA | Consistent placement across the set. |

### Post D — "A December Weekend in Haliburton Before the Snow"

*Why this one:* Targets the early-December gap between the fall-colour season and reliable snow, when the region has less obvious appeal. Aimed at readers with no fixed event in mind.

*Caveat:* Rests on the [INFERRED] premise that early December is a soft period. **Validate against the client's actual occupancy data before investing effort.** This is the weakest of the four and the first to cut.

## 8. Constraints and rules

**Accuracy**
- Never publish a date that has not been confirmed against a primary source for 2026. Prior-year dates are pattern evidence only.
- Do not invent times, prices, vendor counts, attendance figures, parking capacity, or programming details. If it isn't in §2, it isn't known.
- **Accessibility and safety claims carry the highest risk.** The stairs-only note and the Abbey Gardens dark-road advice are the sort of detail that strands or endangers a real person if repeated wrongly. Re-verify or omit.
- Do not present the client as affiliated with, endorsed by, or an official partner of any festival, market, parade or organisation. The relationship is: a local business writing about local events.
- If a date is expected but unconfirmed, say so in the copy ("dates for 2026 hadn't been announced as of [date]") rather than guessing or omitting silently. State the last-checked date on the page.

**Sourcing and copyright**
- Do not reproduce event descriptions verbatim from the tourism site, the festival site, or Facebook. Write original copy from confirmed facts.
- Link out to organisers rather than replacing them. The goal is to be the most useful page, not to absorb the event's own listing.
- Do not reproduce film synopses from festival materials. Titles, runtimes, countries and screening times are facts; the prose around them is not.

**Tone**
- Plain, practical, local. Written by someone who lives there and knows the parking, not by a content mill.
- No manufactured urgency, no superlatives that can't be substantiated ("the region's best market"), no invented tradition or history.
- The commercial ask should be honest and proportionate. The HIFF post earns its CTA because the drive problem is real and festival-acknowledged; don't force that framing where it isn't warranted.
