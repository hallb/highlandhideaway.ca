## 1. Context

### What this is for
A blog post for the website of a short-term rental cottage in Ontario, Canada. The post targets people travelling to the **Corduroy Enduro**, an off-road motorcycle race held in Gooderham, Ontario, and positions the cottage as accommodation for that weekend.

The strategic purpose is twofold:
1. **Capture search demand** for a specific, high-intent, seasonal query set ("where to stay Corduroy Enduro", "Gooderham accommodation", "Haliburton Highlands cottage September"). This kind of proximity-plus-event content is resistant to being fully answered by AI-generated search summaries, because the useful answer is a specific bookable property.
2. **Drive direct bookings** rather than platform bookings, for margin and for conversion tracking.

### Who it's for (audience)
Two distinct reader types, and the post must serve both:
- **Riders** — competitors and their crews. Practical, logistics-driven. Care about trailer parking, bike washing, gear drying, early starts, late returns.
- **Spectators** — partners, families, and friends travelling with riders, plus standalone motorsport fans. Currently under-served by existing content; this is the differentiator.

### Client background (CLIENT-ASSERTED — not independently verified)
The client operates the cottage as a short-term rental, currently distributes primarily through Airbnb, and is building out a direct booking channel. He is comfortable with short-term-rental terminology and is working across both revenue optimisation and direct-booking infrastructure. Treat all of this as background colour, not as fact to publish.

### Current state of the work
- Event facts researched and verified against the official event website (see §2).
- Post structure outlined and reviewed with the client (see §5).
- **Nothing has been drafted.** No copy exists.
- Key property-specific details (cottage name, capacity, amenities, exact drive time) are **not known to the researcher** and must be supplied by the client before drafting.

## 3. Source conflicts and internal inconsistencies

These are unresolved. Do not silently pick a side — resolve them by contacting the organisers or re-checking the site closer to the event.

**A. "3 days" vs. a four-day date range.**
The homepage headlines the event as "3 DAYS, 700 RACERS, $13,500 PRO PURSE" while simultaneously listing the dates as September 17–20, 2026 — a Thursday-to-Sunday span, which is four calendar days. The same page also says racers "battle it out over 2 days" plus a Friday Prologue and Final MX. Three different framings of the event's length appear on one page.
Source: https://www.corduroyenduro.ca
**Action:** Do not state a day-count in the post. Give the date range only, or confirm with the organisers.

**B. "Friday GP" vs. "Promation Prologue" — same thing or two things?**
The homepage lists "Friday GP" as a distinct event component with its own page. The Spectator Info page describes a "Promation Prologue" happening Friday. It is unclear whether these are the same event under two names, two separate Friday events, or a sponsor-name change between page updates.
Sources: https://www.corduroyenduro.ca and https://www.corduroyenduro.ca/blank-5-1-1-2-1-1
**Action:** Unresolved. Check https://www.corduroyenduro.ca/blank-5-1 (the Friday GP page — not fetched during this research) before describing Friday's schedule.

**C. Registration date appears stale.**
The homepage still displays "2026 REGISTRATION OPENS MAY 13, 12:15PM" as a forward-looking announcement, but that date passed roughly three months before this brief was written. This suggests the homepage has not been fully updated since spring 2026.
Source: https://www.corduroyenduro.ca
**Action:** Treat all homepage content as potentially not-current. Re-verify dates and figures before publication.

**D. Copyright year reads 2035.**
The site footer displays "© 2035 Corduroy Enduro" — almost certainly an unedited website-builder placeholder, but it is a further signal that the site is not closely maintained. Do not cite the site as authoritative on anything time-sensitive without a second check.
Source: https://www.corduroyenduro.ca

## 5. Deliverable structure

Working title: **"Where to Stay for the Corduroy Enduro: A Cottage 30 Minutes from Gooderham"**
(Adjust the drive time to the verified figure. Alternative title considered: "Racing or Spectating the Cord? Your Basecamp in the Haliburton Highlands.")

Target queries: *Corduroy Enduro accommodation* / *where to stay Gooderham Ontario* / *Haliburton Highlands cottage September* / *Corduroy Enduro spectator guide*

### Section 1 — Hook (~100 words)
Open on the event's stature, not the property. Lead with the 1953 heritage, the ~700-racer field, the ~300 km of trail, the North American draw. Then pivot to the accommodation problem.
**Reasoning:** A reader searching for this event already cares about the event. Opening with the cottage reads as an advertisement and loses them. Establishing that you understand the race earns the right to pitch. *Note: the scarcity pivot depends on the unverified lodging-supply claim in §4 — verify or rewrite that pivot.*

### Section 2 — The 2026 event details, stated plainly
Dates, location, field size, purse, format. Link out to https://www.corduroyenduro.ca. Keep it short.
**Reasoning:** This block establishes topical relevance and gives the page a reason to rank, but it is also the block most likely to be lifted wholesale into an AI-generated search summary, which produces no click and no booking. So: include it for completeness and credibility, but don't over-invest. The value is in sections 3 and 4, which a summary cannot replicate.

### Section 3 — Why this cottage works as a race basecamp *(primary conversion section)*
Frame every amenity as the solution to a rider's specific problem rather than as a feature list:
- Trailer and truck parking — state length and turnaround honestly
- Somewhere to hose down and stage a filthy bike
- Gear drying space
- A kitchen, for 5am starts and 9pm returns that no restaurant will accommodate
- Sleeping capacity — crews travel as groups and split the cost, which beats per-head motel pricing
- Honest cell and wifi coverage
- Fuel storage policy and location
**Reasoning:** Generic cottage copy ("cosy retreat, lakeside charm") is invisible to this audience. Problem-framed copy signals that the host understands the guest, which is itself a trust signal in a market where riders worry about being turned away for arriving muddy.

### Section 4 — Spectator's guide *(primary differentiator)*
Walk the named spectator points from the official Spectator Info page, adding a practical effort/access note to each. The verified detail for each point is in §2 above.
**Reasoning:** The rider audience is well served by the event's own materials. The people travelling *with* riders are not, and they are frequently the ones actually choosing the accommodation. This section is also the hardest part of the post to replicate, which makes it the section most likely to earn links and repeat visits. Include the caveat that official maps and the full schedule are released closer to the event, and link to the source page.

### Section 5 — Drive times and logistics
Verified drive time to Gooderham, plus nearest fuel, LCBO, grocery, hardware/parts.
**Reasoning:** These are real searches, they are genuinely useful, and they are the kind of specific local detail a general-purpose answer engine gets wrong or omits.

### Section 6 — Make a weekend of it
Late-September shoulder-season framing: quieter, autumn colour beginning, the region at its calmest.
**Reasoning:** Aimed squarely at the non-riding traveller who is being asked to spend a weekend watching motorcycles. Gives them their own reason to say yes. *Constrained by the unverified seasonal claims in §4 — verify colour timing and cut or qualify the swimming claim.*

### Section 7 — Call to action
Direct booking link. Not the Airbnb listing. Offer a discount code (e.g. `CORD26`).
**Reasoning:** Direct booking avoids platform commission and is the only channel where full-funnel conversion tracking is possible. The code does double duty as an attribution mechanism — redemptions attribute cleanly back to this page even when the rest of the funnel is opaque.

### Additions recommended alongside the post
- A short **FAQ block** with schema markup: How far is the cottage from the start? Can I bring a trailer? Is there a minimum stay that weekend?
- **Set the minimum-stay policy for 17–20 September before the page goes live.** A four-night minimum is defensible for an event weekend, but this is a recommendation, not a decision — see §6.

## 7. Constraints and rules

**Do not invent property details.** Capacity, parking dimensions, amenities, drive times and connectivity must come from the client. An inaccurate claim about trailer parking or bike washing will produce an angry guest and a bad review, and those are the exact claims this audience books on.

**Do not overstate connectivity.** Riders and crews follow live timing. Claiming reliable wifi that does not exist is a guaranteed complaint.

**Do not imply affiliation with the Corduroy Enduro.** This cottage is not a sponsor, partner, official accommodation provider, or endorsee. Do not use the event's logo. Do not use phrasing like "official" or "partner". Refer to the event by name descriptively and link to the official site. If in doubt about a phrasing, cut it.

**Do not present the researcher's assumptions as facts.** Everything in §4 flagged as inferred was invented in conversation for rhetorical effect. Publishing "motels fill a year out" or "half the audience are spectators" as fact would be a fabrication.

**Do not copy text from the event's website.** The spectator descriptions in §2 are summaries provided so this brief stands alone. Rewrite them entirely in original wording for the published post.

**Do not give advice about riding, racing safety, or fuel handling.** Stay in the accommodation lane. If mentioning fuel storage, state the property's policy only, and confirm that policy is compatible with the client's insurance before publishing it.

**Be honest about difficulty.** The organisers themselves warn that the hike to Green's Mountain is arduous. Repeat that. A spectator guide that undersells access difficulty and strands somebody's family is worse than no guide.

**Accessibility and weather:** do not promise conditions. Late-September Ontario weather is variable and the post will be live for years.
