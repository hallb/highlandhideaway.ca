## Verified research

### What watercross is
Snowmobiles raced across open water. Sleds reach **north of 100 km/h on the straights**. Sinking is a routine, expected outcome — a local racer interviewed by the Haliburton Echo framed the worst case as simply sinking and having to pull the sled out, which ends that racer's run. Cornering technique is described as similar to motorcycle racing: get low, position on the inside of the sled.

### Puddle jump rules (from OWRA site — distinct discipline within the event)
1. Racer must be 16 or older
2. No flotation on the sled (buoy excepted, and not required)
3. Lifejacket or floater snowmobile suit must be worn
4. Snowmobile helmet at all times when on the sled — colour irrelevant
5. Working tether cord, worn on the racer's wrist whenever the sled is running
6. Brakes in working condition
7. Track in reasonable and safe condition

**Classes:**
- **Stock** — pump gas only, stock clutch cover, max 3 studs per bar (studs optional), working tether, seats and gauges may be removed, twin pipes / aftermarket cans OK provided pump gas. Studs must be carbide-tipped snow studs and must not be sharpened.
- **Mod** — any sled, race or pump gas, stock-or-better clutch cover, chisels allowed with no per-bar limit, 2 skis and 1 track only.
- **No Stud** — any sled with no studs, meeting general plus applicable stock/mod rules, pump or race fuel.

### Local colour
A 2022 Haliburton Echo piece profiled **Tyler Charity**, a Haliburton Highlands Secondary School (Red Hawks) alumnus who graduated in 2017 and races locally. Useful as background texture on the event's local roots — **do not present him as a confirmed 2026 entrant without checking.**
