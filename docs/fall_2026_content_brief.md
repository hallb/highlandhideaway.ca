## POST 1 — The Studio Tour

**Target intent:** Arts and craft travellers researching the tour, plus people searching for fall arts weekends in Ontario.

### Narrative spine

1. **Open with the glass blowing studio.** Owner visited one on last year's tour. This is the hook — sensory, specific, hard to fake. Lead with the heat, the sound, the watching-something-be-made quality. Do not open with generic "Haliburton is a beautiful place to visit."
2. **Broaden to the Haliburton arts scene** — why this area, specifically, has a genuine creative density rather than a manufactured tourist-arts identity.
3. **Fleming College / Haliburton School of Art + Design.** Owner has personally taken a course there. This is credibility, not filler.
4. **What the tour actually involves** — the self-guided format, the range of media, studios tucked into forest and along lakes, the fact that some of these studios are only open these two weekends a year.
5. **CTA** — book the October weekend. Note the Thanksgiving overlap and cross-link to Post 2.

### ⚠️ Factual caution on the art school

The original framing assumed the college *created* the local arts scene. Research suggests the opposite, and this matters for accuracy:

- The **Haliburton School of Fine Arts began in 1967**, with its first offerings held at **Royal View Lodge on Lake Kashagawigamog** — i.e. before it had committed space of its own.
- Late 1967 it was announced it would run as an **Extension Division of Sir Sandford Fleming College**. (Some sources say it *became part of* Fleming in **1969** — these two claims conflict. **Resolve or hedge before publishing.**)
- The campus **moved to Glebe Park, overlooking Head Lake, in 2004**.
- It is now the **Haliburton School of Art + Design**.
- The campus also hosts the **Haliburton Sculpture Forest** (25+ permanent sculptures) — worth mentioning as a free, low-effort activity for guests.

**Recommended framing:** The community came first; the institution grew out of it. This is a *better* story than the original assumption — it makes the local creative identity organic rather than institutional. Write it that way, but don't overstate causality in either direction. Hedged language ("the school grew out of an already-active local arts community") is safer than a hard causal claim.

Source worth reading in full before drafting: `The Story of the Haliburton School of (Fine) Arts` — http://media.hometeamsonline.com/photos/htosports/SDLCA/OCC_success_story.pdf

## POST 2 — Canadian Thanksgiving

**Target intent:** Families and couples searching for a Thanksgiving long weekend getaway in Ontario / cottage country.

### Narrative spine

1. **Open with the thrifting story.** There's a real thrifting scene in Haliburton. On a previous trip the owners found a large Turkish platter. This is the personal hook and it should carry the whole opening — the platter becomes the centrepiece of the Thanksgiving table.
2. **Pivot to the idea of a Haliburton Thanksgiving** as a deliberate escape — someone else's house, no hosting obligations, no cleaning up after twelve people.
3. **Lean into the setting** — harvest season, peak-ish fall colour, the lakes, the slower pace.
4. **CTA** — closing line in the register of *"spend Thanksgiving somewhere worth being thankful for."* (Use as a direction, not necessarily verbatim.)

### Notes
- Cross-link to Post 1 — the Studio Tour runs the same weekend and is a natural add-on activity.
- Keep the thrifting detail concrete. Named shops if the owner can supply them; otherwise keep it impressionistic rather than inventing specifics.

## Rules for drafting

- **First person, genuine experience.** These posts work because they describe things the owners actually did. Do not invent experiences, meals, conversations, or shop names.
- **Anything factual gets re-verified at draft time** — dates, artist counts, studio counts, school history. Several figures above come from sources that disagree with each other.
- **CTA is narrative, not banner-ad.** The pattern is: here's what we did → here's what you could do → book here.
- **Don't merge the two posts.** Different search intent is the entire reason they're separate.
